A machine learning-enhanced decision model turns 15 safety indicators into country rankings, six peer groups, and evidence for targeted transport policy.

Reliability checks

The conclusions remain similar when the method changes.

The paper compares normalization, weighting, aggregation, and clustering alternatives instead of relying on one model run.

What the decomposition reveals

A score becomes useful when its drivers stay visible.

Consistent leaders

The United States, Canada, and Saint Vincent and the Grenadines remained near the top across the decade.

Policy progress

Most countries improved on speed-limit and drink-driving enforcement indicators between the two benchmark years.

Behavioral gap

Front- and rear-seatbelt-use indicators regressed across many countries, revealing a shared intervention target.

Peer learning

Countries within the same cluster still show different strengths, creating practical opportunities for targeted knowledge transfer.

Responsible interpretation

The model supports judgment; it does not replace it.

Weights are objective and do not include stakeholder preferences. The regional dataset depends on secondary sources with uneven completeness, and the clustering still assumes a fixed number of K-means groups. The authors identify hybrid expert weighting and broader regional validation as next steps.
