Three machine-learning models distinguish acute myeloid leukemia (AML) from acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL) and reveal the probes shaping each decision boundary.

Responsible interpretation

Promising classification, with clear validation limits.

The study demonstrates how interpretable machine learning can surface biologically plausible signals without treating a historical, small-sample dataset as clinical proof.

Small n, large p

Seventy-two samples are modest relative to 7,129 probes, increasing overfitting risk.

Historical platform

Microarray measurements may not transfer directly to modern RNA-seq cohorts.

Next validation

Nested cross-validation, external cohorts, and feature-stability analysis are the next steps.
